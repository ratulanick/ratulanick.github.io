About

I am a mechanical engineer with ten years of experience across appliance manufacturing and pharmaceutical production, currently Deputy Manager, Engineering at Renata PLC in Dhaka, Bangladesh. My career moved from refrigeration and HVAC product R&D at Walton Hi-Tech Industries, to cleanroom HVAC and utility engineering at Square Pharmaceuticals, to engineering leadership at Renata, where I manage HVAC, utility and energy systems for a US FDA, UK MHRA and EU GMP-approved facility.

Alongside this industrial career, I completed a Professional Master's in Information Technology at Jahangirnagar University, focused on AI-based predictive maintenance. My project there, an IoT-enabled system for real-time machine health monitoring, grew out of problems I had spent a decade solving on the factory floor, and is now used operationally within my organization, with a patent application in preparation.

My research has since grown from monitoring machine condition toward modelling it, through an AI-driven digital twin framework for predictive maintenance and remaining-useful-life estimation. I am pursuing further doctoral research at this intersection of sensor systems, machine learning and sustainable industrial energy.

Featured Research Project

IoT-Based Industrial Machine Failure Prediction System with Real-Time Notification

A low-cost, multi-sensor predictive maintenance prototype built around an ESP32 microcontroller, developed as my M.Sc. project and validated on live industrial equipment. Now used operationally within my organization, with a patent application in preparation and a digital-twin extension under review at a Q1 journal.

M.Sc. Project

IoT-Based Industrial Machine Failure Prediction System

with Real-Time Notification

Degree: Professional Master's in Information Technology Institution: Institute of Information Technology, Jahangirnagar University Supervisor: Dr. Fahima Tabassum, Professor, IIT, JU Accepted: October 2025

Problem & Motivation

Industrial teams often rely on run-to-failure or fixed-interval maintenance, causing downtime or premature replacement. Commercial predictive-maintenance platforms are expensive and manufacturer-specific, a gap this project addressed with a simple, low-cost, multi-sensor alternative.

Objectives

  • Continuously monitor vibration, acoustic noise and temperature via an ESP32 with dedicated sensors, with a real-time dashboard for live readings.
  • Automatically email maintenance personnel when thresholds are exceeded, and log readings over time for failure-trend analysis.
  • Deliver a cost-effective, scalable full-system prototype.

System Architecture & Technology

An ESP32 microcontroller reads three sensors — an SW-420 (vibration), an LM393 module (acoustic) and a 10k NTC (103) thermistor (temperature) — and sends data over Wi-Fi to the Blynk IoT platform for live dashboards and email alerts, and to Google Sheets via Apps Script for logging. Powered by a single 5V DC / USB-C supply.

Sensing

SW-420 vibration, LM393 acoustic, NTC 103 thermistor

Compute

ESP32 microcontroller, Wi-Fi connected

Platform

Blynk IoT dashboard & email alerts, Google Apps Script → Google Sheets logging

Validation

Tested on real industrial assets — diesel generators, a pharmaceutical blister packaging machine, an air handling unit and an outdoor chiller compressor — confirming correct sensor, dashboard and alert behavior under real operating conditions. Equipment-specific details and photos are withheld as they relate to third-party facilities.

Contribution & Outcome

Delivered a working, low-cost predictive maintenance prototype with real-time alerting and cloud logging, a viable alternative to expensive commercial platforms. Accepted in partial fulfilment of the M.Sc. degree (October 2025), developed further into a conference paper for the 9th European IEOM Conference (2026), and now used operationally within my organization, with a patent application in preparation.

Limitations & Future Work

Limitations include indicative (not reference-grade) sensor readings, no offline logging during Wi-Fi outages, and an enclosure not yet rated for harsh environments. Future work — industrial-grade calibration, offline SD-card logging, SMS alerts, additional sensors, and ML-based adaptive thresholds — continues to shape my current research.
